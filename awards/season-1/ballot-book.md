# Season 1 awards — the ballot book

Every ballot as it was cast, signed by its voter, with their own note on each vote. Voter names and outlets are invented.

## The writers

### Walt "Box Score" Brannigan — The Birdtown Morning Ledger

*Birds chapter, seat 1 · a traditionalist*

**MVP** — 1. Trout (Birds); 2. Soto (Worms); 3. Misiorowski (Birds); 4. Messick (Birds); 5. Melton (Birds); 6. Keith (Birds); 7. Keys (Birds); 8. Clark (Birds); 9. Crow-Armstrong (Birds); 10. Lombard Jr. (Birds)

> I have been filling out ballots the same way for a long time: show me who hit the ball over the fence and who drove the runs home. Trout (Birds) hit 10 home runs when nobody else in the league hit more than 6, drove in 20 when nobody else had more than 15, and was in the lineup for all 29 games of a 21-8 club. That is the Most Valuable Player, and I did not need a slide rule to find him. The man I agonised over was Soto (Worms). A .318 average in a league that hit .219 is a real batting title, and his 35 hits led everybody; I tip my cap, and a batting champion will never finish lower than second on a ballot of mine. But 6 homers and 15 RBI for a club that went 8-21 is a fine season, not the season. Yes, I know Soto's OPS was .849 to Trout's .841. I also know which of them led the league in the two columns I read first.

**Down the MVP ballot**

> After the two bats I went straight to the three Birds arms who each won 5 games - Misiorowski, Messick and Melton - because wins are wins, then to Keith (.272 and 12 RBI playing second base every single day), Keys (5 homers and 14 RBI, and I will forgive the .212), and the legs of Clark (28 steals, a league-best 15 runs), Crow-Armstrong (.273, 18 steals) and Lombard Jr. The one that kept me up was McGee (Birds): a .282 average is my kind of number, but 76 plate appearances falls short of the 90 it takes to qualify, and I could not find room for a part-timer over men who played the whole schedule.

**Cy Young** — 1. Misiorowski (Birds); 2. Messick (Birds); 3. Melton (Birds); 4. Greene (Worms); 5. Duran (Birds)

> Misiorowski (Birds) won the ERA title at 0.98, shared the league lead with 5 wins, struck out a league-high 38 and turned in a quality start in all 8 of his outings. Wins, ERA, strikeouts - that is the old pitching Triple Crown, shared wins column and all, and it makes this the easiest box I ticked. Messick (Birds) was right behind him at 5-3 with a 1.34 and a league-high 67.1 innings, then Melton (Birds) at 5-1, 1.43. I gave Greene (Worms) fourth because a 1.35 ERA and six quality starts in six tries deserve a mention, but I am not handing hardware to a 1-4 record, and I will hear no letters about it. Duran (Birds) gets the fifth line for not allowing an earned run all season.

**Rookie of the Year** — 1. Melton (Birds); 2. Clark (Birds); 3. Lombard Jr. (Birds)

> Melton (Birds) went 5-1 with a 1.43 ERA over 50.1 innings and a quality start every time he took the ball, six for six. A rookie tied for the league lead in wins - I did not have to think about this one for longer than it took to uncap the pen. Clark (Birds) is second for stealing 28 bases and scoring a league-leading 15 runs, though a .224 average keeps a man out of first on my ballot. Lombard Jr. (Birds) is third: .235 with 11 RBI and 13 steals while playing 27 games at shortstop without an error charged to him.

**Manager of the Year** — LinguiniEater over TallThibaut48

> LinguiniEater's Birds went 21-8, won seven of the eight series and outscored the other side 97 to 54. What I liked most is that they were swept 0-3 in the very first series and then did not lose another one - seven straight. They went 12-2 at home, too, which tells me the skipper kept an orderly house. TallThibaut48 gets my second line for that opening sweep as the underdog, the best three days the Worms had all year. The other 26 games are why it is the second line.

**Comeback Player** — McGee (Birds)

> McGee (Birds) hit .205 with a .475 OPS before this league opened and came back to hit .282 with a .776 OPS, a jump of .301 that is the biggest on the board by a mile. He struck out twice in 76 plate appearances, added 6 doubles and 2 triples, and stole 15 bases. Putting the bat on the ball and running hard: I am told this is an old-fashioned way to play. It still works.

**Outstanding DH** — Trout (Birds)

> Trout (Birds) was the designated hitter in all 29 games and led the league with 10 home runs, 20 RBI and 59 total bases. Soto (Worms) took 17 games at DH and spent the other 12 in left field, so he was only a part-time resident of the position anyway. I have my private grumbles about a job where a man never needs a glove, but if the job exists, the fellow who hits 10 homers at it gets the award.

**Relief Pitcher of the Year** — 1. Duran (Birds); 2. Fingers (Worms); 3. Headrick (Worms)

> Duran (Birds) led the league with 3 saves and did not allow an earned run in 10.1 innings - a 0.00 ERA, which is as low as my arithmetic goes. He did blow one save, and I noticed, but he picked up a win as well and struck out 9. Fingers (Worms) is second with 2 saves and a 2.35 ERA, and Headrick (Worms) third with a save, a 1.23 ERA and not one walk issued in 7.1 innings. Saves and ERA - that is how you rank a bullpen, and that is how I ranked this one.

### Dutch Okafor-Lindqvist — The Aviary Evening Ledger

*Birds chapter, seat 2 · a pitching-first voter*

**MVP** — 1. Misiorowski (Birds); 2. Messick (Birds); 3. Trout (Birds); 4. Soto (Worms); 5. Melton (Birds); 6. Greene (Worms); 7. Clark (Birds); 8. McGee (Birds); 9. Crow-Armstrong (Birds); 10. Lombard Jr. (Birds)

> I have said it in this space all summer and I will say it on my ballot: the most valuable thing in this sport is a man who takes the ball and makes the other dugout irrelevant. Misiorowski (Birds) made eight starts and turned in eight quality starts, with a 0.98 ERA over 64.1 innings, a league-high 38 strikeouts and two walks. Two. That is a 19.0 strikeout-to-walk ratio in a league where the rotation rule lets you start a man only once a series, so every one of those turns was a game the Birds could not get back. The agony was Messick (Birds), who actually threw more innings (67.1) and posted the best WHIP in the league at 0.65, and I went back and forth on it over a lot of bad press-box coffee. In the end seven earned runs beats ten, and eight-for-eight beats seven-for-eight. Yes, I put two pitchers ahead of Trout's 10 home runs. Send your letters to the sports desk.

**Down the MVP ballot**

> Trout (Birds) at three and Soto (Worms) at four are the only two bats in this league who cleared an .800 OPS over a full workload, and Soto's .318 batting title on an 8-21 club deserved better than a courtesy mention. I was glad to squeeze Greene (Worms) on at six, because a 1.35 ERA with six quality starts in six tries and a 1-4 record to show for it is the cruelest line in the dossier. The one I could not find room for was Keith (Birds), who hit .272 and soaked up 111 outs at second base, and I owe him a beer.

**Cy Young** — 1. Misiorowski (Birds); 2. Messick (Birds); 3. Melton (Birds); 4. Greene (Worms); 5. Duran (Birds)

> Same argument as the top of my MVP ballot, only with fewer people yelling at me. Misiorowski (Birds) won the ERA title at 0.98, struck out 14 more than Messick (Birds) and never missed a quality start, which outweighs Messick's three extra innings and his 0.65 WHIP. Melton (Birds) at 5-1 with a 1.43 ERA is third, Greene (Worms) is fourth, and I gave my last line to Duran (Birds), who did not allow an earned run in 10.1 innings and led the league with three saves, blown save and all.

**Rookie of the Year** — 1. Melton (Birds); 2. Lombard Jr. (Birds); 3. Clark (Birds)

> Melton (Birds), and I did not need long. A rookie card that goes 5-1 with a 1.43 ERA, a 0.70 WHIP and six quality starts in six outings over 50.1 innings is not a rookie story, it is a front-of-the-rotation story that happens to be new. Lombard Jr. (Birds) gets second for 13 steals and 90 outs at shortstop without an error, and Clark (Birds) third for 28 stolen bases and a league-best 15 runs, which is a lot of havoc from a .224 hitter.

**Manager of the Year** — LinguiniEater over TallThibaut48

> LinguiniEater got swept 0-3 in the opening series and then won the next seven, finishing 21-8 with a plus-43 run differential and a 12-2 record at home. For a club that came in at .689 in the all-time rivalry, a 21-8 season is beating your own standard, not just meeting it, and as a pitching man I will note that his staff allowed 54 runs in 29 games. TallThibaut48 gets second and my sympathy: he won the opener as the underdog, and then went 2-12 on the road.

**Comeback Player** — McGee (Birds)

> McGee (Birds) went from a .205 average and a .475 OPS before the league opened to .282 and .776 in Season 1, a jump of .301 that is the biggest in the dossier by a wide margin. He struck out twice in 76 plate appearances and stole 15 bases while he was at it. I spend my life praising pitchers who miss bats, so it stings a little to salute a hitter who refuses to miss one, but fair is fair.

**Outstanding DH** — Trout (Birds)

> Trout (Birds) was the designated hitter in all 29 games and led the league in home runs (10), RBI (20) and total bases (59), with an .841 OPS in a league that slugged .343 as a whole. Soto (Worms) had the slightly better OPS at .849, but he split his time between DH and left field, and ten of the league's 55 home runs coming off one bat settles it for me. Even a pitching voter knows what the job description says: hit.

### Hollis Brackwater — The Wormtown Daily Castings

*Worms chapter, seat 1 · a volume voter*

**MVP** — 1. Misiorowski (Birds); 2. Messick (Birds); 3. Trout (Birds); 4. Soto (Worms); 5. Melton (Birds); 6. Greene (Worms); 7. Keith (Birds); 8. Clark (Birds); 9. Lombard Jr. (Birds); 10. Crow-Armstrong (Birds)

> I cover the Worms every day, so believe me when I say it gave me no pleasure to hand my first-place vote to the Birds' Misiorowski. But I vote workload, and 64.1 innings at a 0.98 ERA, with a quality start in all eight of his outings, is roughly a quarter of everything the Birds threw all season, done at the best rate in the league. No hitter topped 116 plate appearances; that is not the same size of job. The man I agonised over was his own teammate Messick, who actually out-inninged him, 67.1 to 64.1, with the league's best WHIP at 0.65. Normally three extra innings wins my heart. But Messick gave up 10 earned runs to Misiorowski's 7 and struck out 24 to his 38, and I could not pretend three innings covered that gap.

**Down the MVP ballot**

> Trout's 10 homers and 20 RBI in 116 plate appearances got him third as the top bat, and I was glad to put two of my own Worms in the top six: Soto, who won the batting title at .318 over 112 plate appearances, and Greene, who went 1-4 with a 1.35 ERA and deserves a sympathy card as much as a vote. The rest are Birds who showed up every day: Keith with all 29 games at second, Clark, Lombard Jr. and Crow-Armstrong. The one I could not find room for was the Worms' Baldwin, and before the letters arrive: a 1.097 OPS in 31 plate appearances is a nice fortnight, not a season.

**Cy Young** — 1. Misiorowski (Birds); 2. Messick (Birds); 3. Melton (Birds); 4. Greene (Worms); 5. C. Mize (Worms)

> Same top two as my MVP ballot and for the same reason: Misiorowski's 0.98 ERA and 38 strikeouts against 2 walks beat Messick's extra three innings and 1.34. Melton is third on 50.1 innings at 1.43, and Greene fourth, because six quality starts in six tries at a 1.35 ERA is not diminished by a 1-4 record he did not earn. Fifth was my grumble: C. Mize's 27.2 innings fall just short of the 29 needed for the ERA title, which pains a volume man, but Kluber's bigger pile of 37.2 came at a 3.82 ERA in a 2.58 league, and bulk only counts with me if it is good bulk.

**Rookie of the Year** — 1. Melton (Birds); 2. Clark (Birds); 3. Lombard Jr. (Birds)

> Easiest call on the sheet. Melton made six starts, turned in six quality starts, went 5-1 with a 1.43 ERA over 50.1 innings, the third-heaviest workload in the league from a card nobody had seen before. Behind him I took the two Birds rookies who played nearly every day: Clark, with 114 plate appearances and a league-high 28 steals and 15 runs, and Lombard Jr., 110 plate appearances and 27 games at shortstop without an error in the log.

**Manager of the Year** — LinguiniEater over TallThibaut48

> LinguiniEater went 21-8, took seven of eight series and outscored my club 97-54, and he did it with a lineup you could set your watch by: five Birds played all 29 games. TallThibaut48 gets second and my genuine respect for stealing Series 1 as the underdog with a sweep, but I sat through the rest of it, including nine different cards tried at third base. You cannot build a workload out of auditions.

**Comeback Player** — McGee (Birds)

> The Birds' McGee went from a .205 average and .475 OPS before the league opened to .282 and .776 in Season 1, a jump of .301, the biggest of any hitter in the dossier. He also cut his strikeout rate from 11 percent to 3 and stole 15 bases. Yes, 76 plate appearances is lighter than I like and short of the 90 to qualify for a rate title, but he had 133 beforehand to prove the slump was real, so I am satisfied the turnaround is too.

**Outstanding DH** — Trout (Birds)

> Trout was the DH in all 29 Birds games, which is exactly the kind of attendance record I vote for. He led the league in home runs (10), RBI (20) and total bases (59) on 116 plate appearances with an .841 OPS. My man Soto edged him in OPS at .849, but he only spent 17 games at DH and the rest in left field, so the full-time job goes to the full-time man.

**Relief Pitcher of the Year** — 1. Duran (Birds); 2. Senga (Worms); 3. J. Latz (Worms)

> Duran threw 10.1 innings across six appearances without allowing an earned run, led the league with three saves and added a win; the one blown save is the only smudge. I will admit it nagged at me that the Worms' Senga carried more relief innings than anyone, 14.1 with 14 strikeouts and a 2.51 ERA, but an 0-2 record and four earned runs against Duran's zero settled it. J. Latz is third for 12.1 steady innings and a league-best two holds, which in this bullpen-light league practically makes him an ironman.
